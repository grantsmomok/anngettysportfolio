Medical appointments move fast — especially when you are worried about your child. This list is here to help. Print it out and bring it with you. You do not need to ask everything at once — pick the questions that matter most right now.

Tip

Write down the answers your doctor gives — or ask if you can record the conversation on your phone. It is easy to forget details when you are worried. If you do not understand an answer, it is always okay to say “Can you explain that in simpler terms?”

Before Testing
  • Which genes are being tested — just SFTPC, or a broader panel?
  • Should both parents also be tested for the SFTPC mutation?
  • How long will the results take, and who will explain them to us?
  • Will we need to see a specialist? Who do you recommend?
After Diagnosis
  • What specific mutation does my child have in the SFTPC gene?
  • Was this mutation inherited from a parent, or is it a new (de novo) mutation?
  • What does this specific mutation tell us about what to expect?
  • Should other family members be tested?
  • What is the goal of treatment right now — relieving symptoms, slowing progression, or both?
  • What are the possible side effects of each medication my child is on?
  • Does my child need oxygen? When and how much?
  • What complications should we watch for?
  • What does this diagnosis mean for future pregnancies in our family?
About Breathing Support
  • What type of oxygen equipment does my child need, and when should they use it?
  • Should my child use continuous flow or pulse-dose oxygen?
  • What oxygen level should I aim to keep my child at?
  • Does my child need CPAP or other breathing support during sleep?
  • What training will our family receive before going home with equipment?
At Follow-Up Visits
  • How often does my child need check-up appointments?
  • What tests will track changes in the lungs over time?
  • How will we know if my child’s lungs are getting worse?
  • What symptoms should make me call between visits?
  • What activities are safe for my child right now?
  • Is my child’s growth and nutrition on track?
About School and Daily Life
  • What accommodations does my child need at school?
  • Can you write a letter for the school explaining my child’s needs?
  • What sports or activities can my child safely take part in?
  • Are there any environments my child should avoid — smoke, strong chemicals, crowded spaces?
  • Can you connect us with a social worker or family support resource?
About Research and the Future
  • Are there any clinical trials my child could join?
  • Is there a chILD specialist or research center you would recommend?
  • Are there patient registries we should enroll in?
  • What new treatments are being studied for SP‑C deficiency?
